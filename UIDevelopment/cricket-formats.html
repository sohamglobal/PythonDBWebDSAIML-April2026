Know the game

Common Rules & Terms

Concepts that run across all three formats.

The most common dismissals are Bowled (ball hits the stumps), Caught (a fielder catches the ball before it bounces), LBW — leg before wicket (the ball would have hit the stumps but strikes the batter's leg first), Run Out, and Stumped (the wicketkeeper removes the bails while the batter is out of the crease).

An over is a set of 6 legal deliveries bowled by one bowler. A team's innings ends when it has either used all its available overs (ODI/T20) or lost 10 wickets — or, in Tests, when the captain chooses to "declare."

Runs come from batters running between the wickets, or automatically when the ball crosses the boundary — 4 runs if it touches the ground first, 6 runs if it clears the boundary on the full. Extra runs (wides, no-balls, byes) are added to the team's total.

Each side fields 11 players. Roles include batters, bowlers (pace or spin), all-rounders who do both, and the wicketkeeper, who stands behind the stumps to collect deliveries and attempt stumpings or catches.
Trivia

Did You Know?

The longest Test match ever played lasted 10 days (including rest days) — England vs South Africa, 1939 — and still ended without a result because the ship home couldn't wait any longer.

The first-ever ODI was played in 1971 between Australia and England — purely as a substitute after rain washed out a Test match.

T20 cricket's biggest stage, the IPL, launched in 2008 and is now among the most-watched sporting leagues on the planet.
